Every refusal the eval harness can reach is now the application's own
sentence, and the two it cannot reach say at their definitions why they stay
app-only.

`refusals.ts` exists so the loop and the Node harness turn a tool call away in
the same words. Three of its sentences were pinned — the batch limit, the
mobile shell and the view-only tier — and the harness answered the other two
reachable refusals in wordings of its own: an unmapped tool name got "not on
the allow-list", and a ranked-search call, which the harness offers a model
because the roster it hands a provider is the whole spec table, got the same.
Both now come across the surface entry from the module that owns them, so the
harness answers a missing tool and a missing search exactly as a session does.

That matters more than a tidy set. A refusal is the prompt a case grades a
recovery from, and the missing-tool sentence is the one a run is likeliest to
meet — a model inventing a name is the commonest thing a model gets wrong.
Reworded on one side only, the harness goes on grading against words no
session says and passes while doing it, because the sentence it judges against
is its own. The parity test now pins each new sentence twice: the entry must
re-export it, and a local copy of the text in `run.mjs` fails.

**Two stay app-only, deliberately, and say so.** The sample trial's refusal
needs a session tier no case declares — the harness rehearses every write as a
dry run whether or not a database is configured — and the repeat-read
refusal belongs to a guard the harness's header already declares unmirrored,
since it shapes only what a model sees twice and no case repeats a read.
Sharing either would publish a seam nothing crosses, and a sentence shared
with no reader on the far side cannot be guarded by a test that watches for a
copy: there is no copy to watch for.
