The board's label rail and its three rules read the dial. Four values in the blueprint theme module were still steps of the slate ramp after the overview's four nested surfaces moved onto the semantic ladder: the rail down the left of a blueprint, the panel's interior border, the rules between swim lanes and the phase divider. The rail is a layer, so it takes a rung — `--elevation-4`, the ratio above the one the panel interior sits on, so in dark it is one step brighter than the blueprint it borders and climbs with the rest of the app when a ratio is retuned. Light cannot raise a surface above the card white the panel already is, so there the rail is the board's floor showing through beside the blueprint, the same plate the phase badge sits in and within Δ0.03 of the ramp step it replaces. The rail's hover follows it out by one step instead of jumping to a ramp step. The three rules are edges, and an edge here is an alpha on the foreground: all three read `--border`, the hairline the phase frame already draws its own edge in, so they are neutral in the template, take a deployment's hue and chroma, and flip with the theme without a second declaration. The divider caption's ink and the band it sits on are unchanged, as are every lane-hue cell fill and touchpoint tone.

A deployment that re-tunes the board's chrome reads these names rather than a colour step. The rail is `--background-blueprint-label-rail`, declared per theme, and it stays overridable through `--background-blueprint-panel-label-rail`, which still wins where a deployment stylesheet sets it. The interior border, the lane rules and the phase divider now follow `--border`, so a stylesheet that was pinning them to a grey should turn the border dials — `--contrast-border` and the surface hue and chroma — instead.
